Amasra travel guide

Amasra is a Black Sea town in Bartın province, built on a small peninsula between two harbours. Its ancient name was Sesamos; the town is associated with Queen Amastris, who ruled in the late 4th century BC, and its present name is generally traced to hers. The fortress, the Kemere Bridge, Boztepe and the lighthouse are all reached on foot, and the Roman-era Kuşkayası Road Monument lies close by.

Things to see in Amasra

City centre

Amasra Fortress

Amasra Kalesi

The fortress has two parts: Zindan along the shore and Boztepe on the small peninsula. Founded in Roman times, strengthened by the Byzantines and the Genoese, repaired by the Ottomans. You enter through the Sormagir or the Zindan Gate.

Inside the walls there are still houses, gardens and lanes. Here the fortress is not a museum but a neighbourhood: keep quiet and stay out of the gardens.

Kemere Bridge

Kemere Köprüsü

A single-arched stone bridge joining the two halves of the fortress. Believed to be Roman, repaired in the Byzantine period and restored in 2016.

Look at the relief at its base: sea and wind have worn it away, little by little, for centuries.

Fatih Mosque

Fatih Camii

This building inside the fortress was a Byzantine church. The Genoese used it as their own church; after the conquest it became a mosque.

Look at the stones: the traces of three eras stand side by side in the same wall.

The Church Mosque

Kilise Mescidi

A small place of worship that has served all three eras: Byzantine, Genoese and Ottoman.

In Amasra the stones tell the story of a town that changed hands, and they tell it together.

Amasra Museum

Amasra Müzesi

From Rome to the Ottomans, the changing life of the town under one roof.

As you go round, choose one object: write its name, its era and what it made you think.

The Woodturners' Bazaar

Çekiciler Çarşısı

Woodturning in Amasra passes from father to son. The masters shape lime, boxwood, ash, walnut, cornel and cherry on the lathe and with a knife.

There are fewer masters now, and shops also sell pieces made elsewhere. When you buy a keepsake it is worth asking: "Who made this?"

Boztepe and the Lighthouse

Boztepe ve Deniz Feneri

Cross Kemere and you climb Boztepe; the lighthouse stands at its tip.

From here you can see both waters: most days one is calm and the other choppy.

Bakacak Hill

Bakacak Tepesi

From Bakacak Hill both harbours appear at once.

Here you understand why Amasra is called the eye of the world. Legend says Mehmed the Conqueror asked: "Is this the eye of the world?"

The Two Harbours

Büyük Liman ve Küçük Liman

Two bays on either side of the town: the Big Harbour wide and open, the Small Harbour narrow and sheltered.

Which one has more boats in the morning, and more people in the evening? Walk both.

Rabbit Island

Tavşan Adası

A small island off the shore, best seen from a boat.

Boat trips depend on the weather; ask about the state of the sea.

Near the centre

Kuşkayası Road Monument

Kuşkayası Yol Anıtı

About 4 km outside the town, in the forest, a two-thousand-year-old monument carved into the rock.

Under Claudius (AD 41–54) the governor Gaius Julius Aquila cut a road here at his own expense. The monument bears a human figure, a Roman eagle and a bilingual inscription.

Güzelcehisar Lava Columns

Güzelcehisar Lav Sütunları

Volcanic columns rising from the sea, with a wooden walkway along the shore.

Touch the columns and you touch the surface of lava that cooled millions of years ago.

The Bartın River and City Museum

Bartın Çayı ve Kent Müzesi

The Bartın River runs through the city and into the sea; you can walk its banks or take a boat.

The Bartın City Museum tells of the city's trade, its shipbuilding and its tel kırma embroidery.

Kurucaşile Boatyards

Kurucaşile Tekne Atölyeleri

Kurucaşile is the home of wooden boatbuilding. The masters start from the keel and set the ribs one by one.

Walking past the yards you can watch the masters at work.

Ulukaya Waterfall

Ulukaya Şelalesi

In Ulus, a waterfall pouring out of the mouth of a cave.

Around it are wooden houses and highland pastures; the honey and chestnuts of Ulus come from here.

İnkumu

Bartın's wide beach: busy in summer, quiet in spring.

As you walk, collect what the sea brings in; ask which shell belongs to which creature.

Three Days in Amasra

Day 1 · The fortress and the two harbours

  • Enter the fortress by the Zindan Gate
  • Cross Kemere to Boztepe and walk to the lighthouse
  • The museum and the woodturners' bazaar
  • Sunset from Bakacak

Day 2 · Sea and stone

  • Rabbit Island by boat
  • Kuşkayası Road Monument
  • Late afternoon at Çakraz bay

Day 3 · Towards Bartın

  • The boatbuilders of Kurucaşile
  • The Güzelcehisar lava columns
  • A walk along the Bartın River

Walking and nature routes

Çakraz and Yardibi Bays

Quiet bays following one another east of Amasra: the route for anyone who wants to leave the crowd behind.

Best time: Late afternoon, when the sea settles and the light softens.

Gergece Waterfall

A waterfall in the forest within Amasra's district; the last stretch turns into a footpath.

Best time: The days after rain, when the water runs stronger.

Ahatlar Nature Park

A shaded forest park near Amasra: walking and birdsong.

Best time: Spring and autumn.

Küre Mountains National Park

A national park stretching between Bartın and Kastamonu: canyons, caves and dense forest.

Best time: Late summer and autumn, when the paths are dry.

A short history of Amasra

Amasra's oldest name is Sesamos; Homer mentions it in the Iliad. Around 300 BC Queen Amastris, niece of the Persian king Darius III, united Sesamos with three nearby settlements and gave the new city her name. Pliny the Younger described Amastris in his letters to Emperor Trajan. Fortified by the Byzantines, it became a Genoese trading port in the 13th century, and in the 15th century it surrendered to Mehmed the Conqueror without a battle. Legend says he asked: "Is this the eye of the world?"

Good to know

  • Emergency: 112 (ambulance, police, fire brigade)
  • The town is small; almost all of it can be walked
  • People live inside the walls: keep quiet, stay out of the gardens
  • Boat trips depend on the weather; ask about the sea
  • Do not go deep into forest routes without a guide
  • When buying woodwork, ask where it was made
As the story goes

"Is this the eye of the world?"

Legend says that when Mehmed the Conqueror came to Amasra in 1460 and saw the peninsula, the two harbours and the castle, he called to his tutor: "Lala, lala! Is this the eye of the world?"

Çeşm-i cihan means "the eye of the world". The words have come down as one of the best-known tales about Amasra's beauty, and the town still carries them like a nickname.

Source: Amasra Municipality, town history. The words are passed on as legend.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do I need in Amasra?

The fortress, both harbours, the museum and Boztepe can be walked in a day. A second day suits a boat trip to Tavşan Island, the Kuşkayası Road Monument and Çakraz cove; a third day suits Kurucaşile and Güzelcehisar.

Where does the name Amasra come from?

Around 300 BC Queen Amastris united Sesamos with nearby settlements to found a new city, which took her name.

How do I get to Amasra?

Amasra is reached by road via Bartın; the nearest airport is Zonguldak Çaycuma Airport. Cruise ships also call at the town.

What is Amasra Fortress like?

It has two parts: Zindan along the shore and Boztepe on the small peninsula. It took shape in the Byzantine period, was strengthened by the Genoese and repaired by the Ottomans.

What is the Kemere Bridge?

A historic single-arched stone bridge joining the two parts of the fortress. A worn relief survives on its pier.

What is the Kuşkayası Road Monument?

A few kilometres from Amasra, it marks a road cut into the rock in the reign of the Roman emperor Claudius. It has a carved human figure, an eagle and a Latin-Greek inscription, and is a Roman road monument without parallel in Anatolia.

What is the story of the Fatih Mosque?

Inside the fortress, the building was built as a Byzantine church and converted into a mosque when the town passed to the Ottomans in the 15th century. The story goes that Mehmed the Conqueror looked at the town and asked whether this was the "eye of the world".

What should I eat in Amasra?

In season, anchovy, bonito, bluefish and horse mackerel, with an Amasra salad. Home dishes include stuffed collard greens and ıspıt; for dessert, pumpkin burma. Ulus honey from the Bartın area is well known too.

What is Amasra woodwork?

In the Çekiciler Bazaar, craftsmen turn and carve lime, boxwood and walnut into plates, spoons and small figures. This traditional craft, passed from father to son, is still practised; ask the maker to tell real handwork apart.

What is Bakacak Hill?

A viewpoint over the town, the fortress and the harbours; it is especially lovely at sunset.
