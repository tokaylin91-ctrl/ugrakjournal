Sinop or Amasra?

Short answer: Choose Amasra for a small, fully walkable harbour town and a short getaway. Choose Sinop for a longer stay that combines history, museums and nature routes such as coves, a lagoon and waterfalls. With more time, you can combine both on a road trip following the Black Sea coast; it is a long drive, so don't rush it.

Amasra: two harbours and a fortress

Amasra, in Bartın province, is a small peninsula town whose main sights are reached on foot, with some slopes. The fortress, the Amasra Museum and the Çekiciler bazaar fit into one day. The Kemere Bridge, which links the two parts of the fortress, is generally considered Roman; from it you can walk on to Boztepe and the lighthouse. On a second day you can take a boat to Tavşan Island (weather permitting) and see the Kuşkayası Road Monument, about 4 km outside town.

Sinop: history and nature

Sinop's centre is walkable: fortress walls with roots in the 7th century BC, the Historic Sinop Prison (founded 1887), the Diogenes statue, the Pervane Madrasa and the Alaaddin Mosque. İnceburun, the northernmost point of Anatolia, is also in Sinop. Beyond the centre are Hamsilos cove, often called "Turkey's fjord", the long Akliman beach, the Sarıkum lagoon (protected since 1987) and the Tatlıca Waterfalls at Erfelek, a chain of 28 waterfalls.

Getting there

Sinop has its own airport (NOP, about 8 km from the centre). Amasra is reached by road via Bartın; the nearest airport is Zonguldak Çaycuma Airport.

At a glance

Days: Amasra 1–2, Sinop 2–3. On foot: central Amasra and central Sinop. Nature: coves and forest parks near Amasra; coves, a lagoon and waterfalls around Sinop. Best for: Amasra suits a short, quiet getaway; Sinop suits travellers who want history and nature together.

Opening hours, transport and event details can change; check with the relevant institution before you go.
