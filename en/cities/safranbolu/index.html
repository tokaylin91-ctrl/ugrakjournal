Safranbolu travel guide

Safranbolu, in Karabük province, is an Ottoman town whose timber mansions, stone inn and living bazaar have been on the UNESCO World Heritage List since 17 December 1994. The town has three parts: Çarşı, Kıranköy and Bağlar. Highlights include Cinci Inn (1645), the craftsmen of the Arasta, the rooftop view from Hıdırlık and the İncekaya Aqueduct over the Tokatlı Canyon.

Last checked: 27 November 2026 · By Uğrak Journal, Sinop · How we research

Things to see in Safranbolu

Çarşı

The Safranbolu House

Safranbolu Evi

Safranbolu's houses are its identity: a stone ground floor, timber storeys above, bay windows over the street and many windows.

The Kaymakamlar House is known as one of the finest examples and is visited as a house museum; check it is open before you go. Inside, look at the layout of the rooms and the woodwork: they tell you how a house was lived in.

Cinci Inn

Cinci Hanı

A stone caravanserai built for Cinci Hoca (Kazasker Hüseyin Efendi) in the reign of Sultan İbrahim.

Sources date it to 1645. Sit for a while in its courtyard, where caravans rested for centuries.

Cinci Bath

Cinci Hamamı

A bath built by the same patron and dated to the same period as the inn.

Together, inn and bath tell of daily life in a town on the caravan route: travellers rested first, then bathed.

Köprülü Mehmet Pasha Mosque

Köprülü Mehmet Paşa Camii

Built by the grand vizier Köprülü Mehmet Pasha, the mosque opened for worship in 1661.

It stands in the middle of the bazaar, sharing the same streets with shops for centuries. When you visit, respect prayer times.

İzzet Mehmet Pasha Mosque

İzzet Mehmet Paşa Camii

İzzet Mehmet Pasha, a grand vizier born in Safranbolu, built the mosque in the bazaar that bears his name.

The town carries the marks of a grand vizier from among its own people: the same pasha also built the clock tower and the İncekaya Aqueduct.

The Clock Tower

Saat Kulesi

The clock tower was built in 1797 by İzzet Mehmet Pasha of Safranbolu, grand vizier to Selim III.

It stands on the hill called the castle, looking down on Çarşı. Stand in front of it and set your watch by the tower.

Safranbolu Museum

Safranbolu Müzesi

Next to the clock tower, the old Government House was built between 1904 and 1906.

Restored after a fire in 1976, it opened in 2007 as the City History Museum and today serves as the Safranbolu Museum. Inside you can explore the town's past, its traditional life and its crafts.

The Arasta

Arasta

The Arasta is the craftsmen's arcade of Çarşı: shops stand side by side, each devoted to one trade.

It is still a living bazaar where people shop. Walk slowly and look at the workbench more than the window.

Masters of the Bazaar

Çarşının Ustaları

In Safranbolu you can see traces of traditional crafts such as yemeni shoemaking, blacksmithing, coppersmithing and saddle-making. Some craftsmen still keep them alive today.

If you get the chance to watch a craftsman at work, take it. Handmade pieces carry small differences.

The Town from Hıdırlık

Hıdırlık'tan Kent

From Hıdırlık Hill the rooftops of Çarşı are seen all together.

Red tiles, white walls, minarets and the clock tower: the town can be read at a single glance.

Kıranköy and Bağlar

Kıranköy

Kıranköy is one of the historic quarters where a Greek Orthodox community once lived. The 1924 population exchange greatly changed its social fabric.

As you walk its streets, listen to the language of the houses. People live here today; respect their private spaces.

Bağlar

Bağlar is the town's old summer quarter: houses with gardens, trees and water.

Traces of a life lived in Çarşı in winter and in Bağlar in summer can still be read today.

Canyon

İncekaya Aqueduct

İncekaya Su Kemeri

İzzet Mehmet Pasha built the aqueduct across the Tokatlı Canyon in the 18th century.

It has six arches, one main and five smaller, and rises up to about 60 metres above the canyon. Keep away from the edge; children should stay with an adult.

The Crystal Terrace and the Tokatlı Canyon

Kristal Teras ve Tokatlı Kanyonu

Near İncekaya, the Crystal Terrace is a glass terrace looking down on the canyon from about 80 metres.

Look at the drop beneath your feet, then at the slope opposite. The Tokatlı stream runs along the bottom of the canyon. Children should go onto the terrace with an adult; follow the staff's instructions.

A short history of Safranbolu

Safranbolu lies within the historic region known in antiquity as Paphlagonia; the town's past goes back thousands of years.

In 1196 it came under Turkish rule in the time of Melik Muhiddin Mesut, son of the Seljuk sultan Kılıç Arslan II; later it was held by beyliks such as the Çobanoğulları and the Candaroğulları, and in Ottoman times it grew into an important centre of settlement and trade.

The town's name is generally linked with the saffron plant grown in the area; in Ottoman times it was also called "Zafranbolu".

With its wooden mansions, stone inn and living bazaar, it has been on the UNESCO World Heritage List since 17 December 1994.

Good to know

  • Emergency: 112 (ambulance, police, fire brigade)
  • Çarşı is explored on foot; the streets are cobbled and steep
  • Wear comfortable shoes
  • Some mansions are still homes; keep your voice down
  • Keep away from the edge in the canyon and on terraces
  • When buying handwork, ask where it was made
As the story goes

The night builders of Cinci Inn

A tale told in Safranbolu says that while Cinci Inn was being built, the masons went home each evening. Yet tapping sounds were heard all night, and each morning the wall stood higher than they had left it.

Everyone said the djinn were building the wall. At last a few masons hid at night to solve the mystery. The builders were not djinn but the women of the village: the men worked by day, the women by night.

Those who tell it say the inn rose through the shared work of women and men. Historical records note that the inn was built for Cinci Hoca in 1645.

Source: Kudret Ayşe Yılmaz, Türk Dili magazine, May 2025. A folk tale, not a historical record.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do I need in Safranbolu?

Çarşı can be seen in a day. With three days: Cinci Inn, the Arasta and Hıdırlık on day one; the clock tower, Safranbolu Museum, the Kaymakamlar House and Kıranköy on day two; İncekaya, the Crystal Terrace and Yörük Village on day three.

Where does the name Safranbolu come from?

The name is generally linked to the saffron crocus grown in the area; in Ottoman times the town was also called "Zafranbolu".

When did Safranbolu join the UNESCO list?

It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List on 17 December 1994.
