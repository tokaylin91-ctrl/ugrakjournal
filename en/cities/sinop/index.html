Sinop travel guide

Sinop is a harbour town on a peninsula on Turkey's Black Sea coast, whose story goes back to the founding of ancient Sinope in the 7th century BC. The philosopher Diogenes was born here; the fortress walls, the Historic Sinop Prison and İnceburun, the northernmost point of Anatolia, are its landmarks. Beyond the centre lie the fjord-like Hamsilos cove, Akliman beach and the Tatlıca Waterfalls of Erfelek, a chain of 28 waterfalls.

Things to see in Sinop

City centre

Sinop Fortress

Sinop Kalesi

Its roots go back to the 7th century BC. The walls wrap around the city for about 2 kilometres and rise up to 25 metres in places. Pontics, Byzantines, Seljuks and Ottomans all repaired and used it.

Historic Sinop Prison

Tarihi Sinop Cezaevi

Inside the inner fortress, right by the sea. Its foundations were laid as a prison in 1887. The writer Sabahattin Ali was held here, and his famous lines were written behind these walls.

Statue of Diogenes

Diyojen Heykeli

The philosopher Diogenes was born in Sinop around 412 BC. Legend says he lived in a barrel. When Alexander the Great asked if he wanted anything, he replied: "Stand out of my sunlight."

Pervane Madrasa

Pervane Medresesi

Built in the 1260s by the Seljuk vizier Muinüddin Süleyman Pervane. Today this open-courtyard madrasa is a quiet historic building you can walk around.

Alaaddin Mosque

Alaaddin Camii

After Sinop came under Seljuk rule in 1214, the church was turned into a mosque. Its tomb-lined courtyard is a shady, quiet corner.

Balatlar Church

Balatlar Kilisesi

Locals call it a church, but excavations revealed a Roman group of buildings with baths and sacred structures. In Byzantine times it was also used as a monastery.

Ethnography Museum

Etnografya Müzesi

The three-storey wooden Arslan Torun Mansion, from the 19th century. Wander its rooms and you become a guest in an old Sinop home.

Tomb of Seyit Bilal

Seyit Bilal Türbesi

One of Sinop's oldest tombs, rebuilt in the 13th century after the Seljuks took the town.

Naval Martyrs Monument

Deniz Şehitleri Anıtı

Built for the sailors who lost their lives in the 1853 Battle of Sinop. It stands in the garden of the Archaeology Museum and opened in 1933.

Dr. Rıza Nur Kütüphanesi

The building dates from 1902 and has been a library since 1924. Rıza Nur's books, gathered from around the world, are kept here by the sea.

İnceburun

The northernmost tip of Anatolia. Standing in front of the lighthouse, you feel like you're at the end of the world. Perfect with a glass of tea and the sound of the waves.

Near the centre

Hamsaroz (Hamsilos)

The sea curls in between forested hills; this fjord-like cove is a protected natural site of the highest degree.

Sinop Arkeoloji Müzesi

Since 1941 it has kept what Sinop's soil gives up: amphorae, coins, Byzantine icons. In the garden, the remains of a temple of Serapis are waiting for you.

Paşa Bastions

Paşa Tabyaları

A half-moon coastal bastion built in the 19th century to guard the harbour and shipyard; eleven cannon emplacements face the sea. Access may change; if it's closed, enjoy it from the shore.

Boyabat Castle

Boyabat Kalesi

High on a steep 135-metre rock in the Gökırmak Valley. It bears Roman, Byzantine and Ottoman traces; tunnels and stairways carved into the rock have also been found.

Durak Han (Caravanserai)

Durak Hanı

Built in 1266 by the Seljuk vizier Muinüddin Süleyman Pervane. With its open courtyard and vaulted hall, this caravanserai still stands after its 2007 restoration.

Walking and nature routes

Hamsaroz (Hamsilos) Bay

A narrow, winding cove where the forest meets the sea. Its shape has earned it the nickname "Türkiye's fjord". Together with Akliman, it became a nature park in 2007.

Akliman Beach

A long beach much loved by the people of Sinop. Dunes, wetland and forest meet here. Together with its neighbour Hamsaroz (Hamsilos), it is a nature park.

Sarıkum Nature Reserve

A lagoon lake cut off from the sea by a strip of dunes. A nature reserve since 1987. Nearly 50 bird species can be seen on and around the lake.

Tatlıca Waterfalls, Erfelek

28 waterfalls follow one another along the Gülleyük stream, stepping down a valley about 4.5 km long. Wooden stairs and paths lead from one fall to the next.

A short history of Sinop

Sinop's story begins with the sea. In the 7th century BC, sailors from Miletus founded a colony at this sheltered harbour; the philosopher Diogenes was born here. In 183 BC it became the capital of Pontus; Roman and Byzantine times followed. The Seljuks came in 1214, then the Candarids, and in 1461 the Ottomans… The fortress walls carry all of these layers.

  • 7th c. BCSailors from Miletus found a colony here.
  • Late 5th c. BCThe philosopher Diogenes is born in Sinope.
  • 183 BCPharnaces I takes the city; it becomes Pontus's capital.
  • 70 BCThe Roman general Lucullus captures the city.
  • 1214Sultan Kaykaus I brings Sinop under Seljuk rule.
  • 1461Sinop joins the Ottoman Empire under Mehmed the Conqueror.
  • 1853Battle of Sinop: the Ottoman squadron in the harbour is destroyed.
  • 1887The prison inside the fortress opens; it is used until 1997.
  • TodayA calm coastal town of museums, harbour and good food.
As the story goes

Sinope's wish

In ancient tales, Sinope was a water nymph, daughter of the river god Asopos. Zeus promised to grant her greatest wish.

Sinope wished to live as she chose, without marrying anyone, and Zeus kept his word. The tale says Apollo and the river god Halys made her the same promise, and each time Sinope held to her own choice.

In ancient Greek tales, Sinope's name is linked with this city on the Black Sea coast.

Source: Apollonius of Rhodes, Argonautica (3rd century BC). This page is an adaptation of the myth for young readers; in the original the wish is worded differently.

Frequently asked questions

What are the main sights in Sinop?

Sinop Fortress and its walls, the Historic Sinop Prison, the Diogenes statue, Pervane Madrasa, Alaaddin Mosque, Balatlar Church and İnceburun, the northernmost point of Anatolia. Near the centre are Hamsilos cove and the Sinop Archaeological Museum.

What is Hamsilos (Hamsaroz)?

A narrow, winding cove where the forest meets the sea, often called "Turkey's fjord". Together with Akliman it became a nature park in 2007.

How do I get to Sinop?

Sinop Airport (NOP) is about 8 km from the centre and the bus station about 7 km. The centre is walkable; the districts are reached by car or bus.
