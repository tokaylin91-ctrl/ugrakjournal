Cappadocia travel guide

Cappadocia is known for its fairy chimneys, carved by wind and water out of soft tuff rock formed from volcanic ash. This guide focuses on Göreme and Uçhisar: the Göreme Open-Air Museum with its rock-cut churches, Uçhisar Castle, and the Pigeon, Love, Rose, Red and Zemi valleys. "Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of Cappadocia" has been on the UNESCO World Heritage List since 1985.

Things to see in Cappadocia

Göreme

Göreme Open-Air Museum

Göreme Açık Hava Müzesi

About 2 km east of the village: a monastic settlement carved into the rock, used from the 4th to the 13th century, with 17 churches and chapels.

The images were made in two ways: paint straight onto prepared rock, or fresco on plaster. Moving from one church to another, you pass through a silence that has lasted for centuries.

Tokalı Church and the Deep Blue

Tokalı Kilise ve Koyu Mavi

One of the largest and earliest known rock churches in the region, from the 10th century: the Old Church, the New Church, the Lower Church and a side chapel.

What sets the New Church apart is the deep blue of its frescoes. The scenes tell Gospel stories and read in order from the right wing to the left: the wall is a book.

The Dark Church

Karanlık Kilise

The Dark Church is named for the single small window in its narthex, which lets in very little light.

That little light has kept its colours for centuries, so its 11th–12th century paintings are among the best preserved in the region.

The Apple, Snake and St Barbara Churches

Elmalı, Yılanlı, Azize Barbara

The Apple Church has four columns in a closed Greek-cross plan, with Christ Pantocrator in the dome.

The Snake Church takes its name from a wall scene: St George fighting a serpent. In St Barbara Church you see geometric patterns rather than figures.

Göreme Village and its Names

Göreme Köyü ve Adları

In 451 the records of the Council of Chalcedon mention two neighbouring villages, Korama and Matiana: Korama stood where the open-air museum is today, Matiana where the village is.

The village has also been known as Avcılar and Göreme. Legend says the name goes back to people sheltering in the valleys who said "gor emi", "you cannot see this place". People live in the village; keep your voice down.

Balloons at Sunrise

Gün Doğumunda Balonlar

Cappadocia's mornings fill the sky: at sunrise hundreds of balloons rise over the valleys.

You do not need to fly: climb a hill and watch the light and the balloons. Balloons depend on the weather.

Uçhisar

Uçhisar Castle

Uçhisar Kalesi

The highest rock mass in the region, with the village at its foot. It is honeycombed with connected rooms, tunnels and storerooms, most of them closed today.

People are said to have lived inside until recent times. Beneath the village runs a tunnel of about 100 metres, thought to have linked the castle to the outside and protected its water.

The View from Uçhisar

Uçhisar'dan Manzara

Climb to the top and take in the landscape from above: valleys, fairy chimneys, villages in the distance.

On a clear day you can pick out Erciyes, one of the volcanoes that shaped this land, on the horizon.

Valleys

Pigeon Valley

Güvercinlik Vadisi

Between Uçhisar and Göreme, about 4 km long, named for the hundreds of pigeon houses carved into the rock.

People carved homes for the pigeons, and pigeon manure was traditionally used to fertilise the vineyards and fields, until recent times. Some pigeon houses even contain frescoes.

Love Valley (Bağlıdere)

Aşk Vadisi (Bağlıdere)

Known for its tall, slender fairy chimneys, west of the Göreme–Çavuşin road.

It can be walked as far as Uçhisar. We give no times or distances: ask a local before you set out.

Rose and Red Valleys

Güllüdere ve Kızılçukur

Both valleys hold frescoed churches and pigeon houses.

Both are known for their sunsets; do not miss the late-afternoon light.

Swords Valley

Kılıçlar Vadisi

It runs down from above the open-air museum and ends at Çavuşin.

As you walk between the sharp rock ridges, think about how the valley got its name.

Zemi Valley and El Nazar Church

Zemi Vadisi ve El Nazar Kilisesi

Zemi Valley starts between the museum and the village and climbs to El Nazar Church.

The church is carved into a free-standing mass of rock.

Walking and nature routes

The Pigeon Route

From Uçhisar to Göreme, among the pigeon houses.

Best time: Morning, when the light falls across the valley.Practical tip: Ask a local about the path before you set out and bring water.

Best time: Before noon, while the valley is still cool.Practical tip: Ask a local about the path before you set out and bring water.

A short history of Cappadocia

This land was shaped by fire first. Ash and lava from Erciyes, Hasandağı and Göllüdağ left a soft rock: tuff. Wind and water wore it down for millions of years, and where harder rock capped softer layers, fairy chimneys remained.

The region's old name is Katpatuka. Christianity spread under Rome, and from the 4th century Cappadocia became a place of retreat for monks.

Because tuff is easy to carve, people cut homes, churches, storerooms and pigeon lofts into the rock.

Since 1985, "Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of Cappadocia" has been on the UNESCO World Heritage List.

As the story goes

The Three Beauties

Near Ürgüp, three fairy chimneys are known as the Three Beauties. People who see the two tall rocks and the small one imagine a family: mother, father and child.

The likeness has become one of Cappadocia's best-loved tales. The chimneys are soft tuff; even a scratch stays for centuries. Leave the view as it is, without writing on the rock.

This is a folk interpretation; the rocks were shaped by natural erosion.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do I need in Göreme and Uçhisar?

Three days is comfortable: balloons at sunrise and the Open-Air Museum on day one, the Pigeon Valley walk to Uçhisar and its castle on day two, and Love Valley with a quiet village afternoon on day three.

Do the balloons fly every day?

No. Balloon flights depend on the weather.

How were the fairy chimneys formed?

Ash and lava from Erciyes, Hasandağı and Göllüdağ left a soft rock called tuff. Wind and water wore it down over millions of years; where hard rock sat on top of soft rock, fairy chimneys remained.
